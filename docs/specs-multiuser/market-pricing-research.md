# Market Pricing Research: Voice-First AI MCAT Oral Examiner

**Researched:** 2026-08-10, via live web search/fetch (vendor pages where reachable, review aggregators otherwise). Prices are US retail; most vendors run frequent promos, so treat these as list-price anchors.

**Product being priced:** voice-first AI MCAT oral examiner — adaptive drilling, episodic memory of the student's mistakes, photo-of-question ingestion, spaced repetition. Marginal cost is realtime voice audio, ~$2–6 per hour of active conversation.

## 5. Marginal cost floor: realtime voice API (OpenAI gpt-realtime, mid-2026)

- gpt-realtime-2.1: $32/M audio input tokens, $64/M audio output tokens. Audio ≈ 600 tokens/min heard + 1,200 tokens/min spoken → raw ≈ $0.10/min ($6/hr) worst case; **with prompt caching working, measured real-world agents run $0.06–0.11/min ($3.60–6.60/hr)**; mini model $0.02–0.05/min ($1.20–3/hr).
- Without caching, long sessions can spike to $0.18–0.46/min — session/context management is itself a margin lever.
- Working planning number: **$2–6/hour of active conversation** (mini-with-caching to full-model-typical). Text-side costs (grading, memory writes, spaced-rep scheduling) are noise by comparison.
- Sources: https://hackernoon.com/openai-realtime-api-pricing-in-2026-real-world-data-from-4000-measured-sessions , https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/openai-realtime-api-pricing , https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gpt-realtime-mini-pricing

## 6. Analysis: where a voice-AI oral examiner fits

### The three anchors that matter

1. **Human oral tutoring: $150–250/hr effective** (Wyzant credible-tutor range; Blueprint $225/hr; Princeton $177–330/hr). This is what the product substitutes. One hour of AI examiner at $2–6 cost delivers the drilling function of a session students pay $200 for. This anchor justifies premium pricing per hour of use — a 97%+ discount to the substitute still leaves 10–30x gross margin.
2. **UWorld QBank: $339/90 days.** The reference price for "a serious, indispensable MCAT tool with time-boxed access." Students who balk at a $1,500 course pay this without blinking. A 90-day pass in the $199–399 band reads as "UWorld-priced," i.e., a must-have tool, not an app.
3. **Consumer voice-AI apps: $8–20/mo (Speak, Praktika, ChatGPT Plus) and Khanmigo at $4/mo.** This is the anchor to *avoid* — if the product is framed as "an AI voice app," willingness-to-pay collapses to ~$20/mo, which unlimited use can actually lose money on (20 hrs × $6 = $120 cost).

### Positioning options

- **(A) Standalone premium tool, UWorld-style pass** — sold like a QBank: 30/90/180-day access aligned to test dates. Cleanest fit with category buying behavior; students assemble stacks (AAMC $324 + UWorld $339 + course), and a $199–349 pass slots into an existing $2,000–3,500 budget line. Recommended primary.
- **(B) Tutoring substitute, sold in hours** — "oral exam hours" packs priced against $200/hr humans (e.g., 10 AI hours for $99 vs $2,000 human). Strong value story, but per-hour framing invites cost-comparison to the API floor if it ever leaks, and hour-packs feel scarce, suppressing the daily-drilling habit spaced repetition needs.
- **(C) White-label/add-on to an incumbent** — UWorld and Blueprint both bundle text-AI free; neither has voice. A voice examiner is a differentiator they could pay per-seat for (B2B: $30–60/seat/90-days at volume). Worth pursuing in parallel; incumbents' free-AI bundling also means a standalone must stay clearly *beyond* what their included tutors do (voice, memory, oral-exam format).

### The unit-economics constraint (why flat unlimited is dangerous)

At $2–6/hr marginal cost, a $20/mo unlimited plan is underwater at 4–10 hrs/month of use — and MCAT crunch-phase students study 20–40 hrs/week. Even a $99/mo unlimited plan loses money on a 3 hr/day power user in month before test day ($90 hrs × $4 ≈ $360). Every price below must carry a minute mechanism.

**Three mechanisms:**
- **Monthly minute buckets** (included minutes, top-ups at ~$0.15–0.25/min ≈ 2.5–4x cost): predictable revenue, margin-safe, top-ups monetize crunch mode.
- **Time-boxed pass + fair-use cap** (e.g., "90-day pass, up to X min/day"): matches category norms; a daily cap (e.g., 45–60 min/day) reads as pedagogy ("focused daily orals") rather than meanness, and bounds worst-case cost tightly.
- **Pure metered** ($/min): maximal margin safety, worst psychology — meters suppress usage, and the product's value (episodic memory, spaced repetition) compounds with *daily* use.

### Proposed price points with margin math

Cost basis: blended $0.05/min ($3/hr) — full model with caching, mini for drills. Pessimistic case $0.10/min in parentheses.

**P1 — "Examiner Pass" (primary): $299 / 90 days, fair-use 60 min/day.**
- Anchored 1:1 against UWorld's $339/90-day QBank; also 30-day $129, 180-day $449.
- Expected use: median student ~15 min/day × 75 active days ≈ 19 hrs → cost ~$56 ($113 pessimistic) → **~81% gross margin (62% pessimistic)**.
- Absolute worst case (60 min × 90 days = 90 hrs): cost $270 ($540) → breakeven at blended cost; the daily cap is what makes the worst case survivable, and real usage distributions make it rare. If pessimistic cost dominates, cap at 45 min/day.

**P2 — "Monthly + minutes": $79/mo with 500 min (8.3 hrs), top-ups $10/100 min.**
- Anchored above Speak/ChatGPT ($20) to escape the app reference class, far below any course. Full bucket consumption costs $25 ($50) → **68% margin (37% pessimistic)**; top-ups run 50–75% margin. Suits early-phase students not yet on a test-date countdown; upgrade path into P1.

**P3 — "Tutor-substitute premium": $699 / 180 days, 90 min/day cap, includes photo-ingestion of any QBank question + full episodic-memory review dossier.**
- Anchored against 10 hrs of human tutoring ($2,000–3,300) and Kaplan/Blueprint course tiers ($1,099–1,599): "a private oral examiner for six months for a third of a course." Heavy user at 45 min/day × 150 days = 112 hrs → cost $337 ($675) → **~52% margin** (breakeven pessimistic — price the cap accordingly or gate the full model to graded mock orals and run drills on mini).

**Margin lever worth engineering, not just pricing:** route routine drilling to gpt-realtime-mini ($1.20–3/hr) and reserve the full model for graded mock oral exams; with caching discipline this pulls blended cost toward $0.03/min and adds ~15 points of margin at every tier.

### Recommendation

Lead with **P1 ($299/90-day pass, 60 min/day fair-use)** as the flagship — it matches how the category buys (time-boxed, test-date-aligned), borrows UWorld's price legitimacy, and the daily cap bounds voice cost while framing as pedagogy. Keep **P2** as the low-commitment entry/upgrade funnel. Hold P3 until usage data proves the heavy-user cost distribution. Never ship unlimited flat pricing at any tier.
